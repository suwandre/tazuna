Points a good answer covers: under the common-law mirror-image rule the reply adds a new term, so it is a counter-offer that rejects the original offer, not an acceptance; the seller's silence is not acceptance; so no contract, and the seller was free to sell elsewhere. For a sale of goods in the US, UCC 2-207 applies: a definite expression of acceptance can operate as acceptance even with additional terms (unless expressly conditional on assent to them); between merchants the added term becomes part of the contract unless it materially alters it, the offer limits acceptance to its terms, or the seller objects in time. A delivery deadline may well be a material alteration, in which case the contract forms on the seller's terms without it. It should note this is general information, not legal advice for a specific case.
